How to grade a card in 60 seconds

  1. Good light, no sleeve. Take the card out, tilt it under a lamp or daylight. Look at the front, then the back — the back shows wear first on vintage cards.
  2. Corners (4). Sharp points = NM. Tiny white tick = EX. Visibly rounded = LP or worse.
  3. Edges. Look for whitening along the back border (the blue border shows it clearly on WotC cards) and small nicks.
  4. Surface. Tilt the holo under light: scratches, scuffs, print lines, loss of gloss. Check for dents or indents by looking across the card at a low angle.
  5. Bends & creases. Lay it flat. Any crease (a line that breaks the surface) caps the grade at PL. Any writing, tear or water damage = PO.
  6. When in doubt, grade one step lower. Buyers forgive an undergraded card; they return an overgraded one.

The 7 grades below follow the Cardmarket scale (the marketplace French collectors use). Multipliers are applied to the Near-Mint market price to estimate what each condition sells for; they are typical ratios, not guarantees.

Things that change the price a lot

1st Edition stamp (small "1" badge left of the illustration, French Set de Base, Jungle, Fossile, Team Rocket, Gym, Neo): typically 2–5× the unlimited price for holos. Tick "1st Ed." on the card.

Holo vs non-holo: the same Pokémon can exist as a holo (rare, high value) and a non-holo in the same set — check the card number.

Language: reference prices come from Cardmarket (all languages mixed, mostly English). French copies of common cards often sell a bit lower; French chase cards (Dracaufeu, Tortank, Florizarre…) sell close to English. Use the "French price adjustment" slider in Export to apply a global correction once you see real sale results.

Centering & print quality: off-centre borders or print lines lower a grade for picky buyers but don't change the condition code.
